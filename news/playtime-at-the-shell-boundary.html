PlayTime as a shell-boundary cousin

A document runner that spawns argv without a shell rhymes with OpenShellOrg. It is being built in Dev-Centr scriptbook. We are not moving it.

Dev-Centr is teaching a document to play.

Not tangle source (that is their literate-programming track). Play: install this, check that, on this computer, without rewriting the file. The engine in the middle picked up a name from the phrase "at play time" — PlayTime.

That engine does something we already preach. It treats an install method as argv, not as a bash costume and a pwsh costume and a nu costume hanging on the same hook. It snapshots the host before it asks a catalog anything, because "what OS am I?" cannot be an equivalence lookup that requires knowing the OS.

That is shell-boundary work. Entrypoint Dispatch, SOS, structured pipelines — different layers, same allergy to lying about what process you just started.

We wrote the cousin essays on our side so CLI authors do not have to archaeology the Dev-Centr specs:

  • PlayTime at the shell boundary — why it sounds like us, why it is not in this org yet

  • Argv as execution currency — format is not a shell; never ask "MSI or bash?"

  • Host identity as a contract — no world get_os(); overlays are stickers, not planets

We are not merging, and we are not relocating their kitchen mid-service. PlayTime is being built in dev-centr/scriptbook. If it ever moves, argv-spawn and Host Awareness should come along. Their tool catalogs and GUI should not.

Their inward tour: Intents, not shell translation. Encyclopedia: Abstract playbooks.
